# Daily quiz: all 50 questions

For review. This file is generated from the database (`quiz_questions` table); the source is `js/data.js`. The correct answer is marked **✔**. Questions 1-10 are the original set, 11-50 are new. One question is shown per day, in this order, and the list repeats after 50 days.

### 1. Why does CoffeeBot dry the grounds before sending them to your school?

- A) To make them smell nicer
- **✔ B) Wet grounds can grow mould**
- C) To change their colour

> Damp grounds trap moisture, and mould loves that. Drying keeps them fresh and safe to use.

### 2. What is a good sign your kit grounds are still fine to use?

- A) They clump together when squeezed
- **✔ B) They feel crumbly and smell normal**
- C) They look fuzzy

> Crumbly means no water has got in. Fuzzy or damp grounds mean mould, so tell your teacher.

### 3. Where should you keep your kit container?

- A) Somewhere damp
- **✔ B) A dry spot with the lid closed**
- C) In the sun with the lid off

> Keeping the lid on and the spot dry stops moisture getting back in.

### 4. Used coffee grounds added to soil mostly give plants…

- **✔ A) Nitrogen**
- B) Sunlight
- C) Salt

> Grounds are a source of nitrogen, a nutrient plants use to grow leaves.

### 5. How much grounds should you mix into soil for a seedling?

- A) All grounds, no soil
- **✔ B) About one part grounds to four parts soil**
- C) None, never mix them

> Too much can pack down and hold water. A small share works best.

### 6. Why do coffee-ground sachets help a smelly shoe?

- **✔ A) Dried grounds soak up smells**
- B) They make the shoe warmer
- C) They add a strong perfume

> Dried grounds are full of tiny pores that trap smelly molecules, so they absorb odours instead of covering them up.

### 7. What happens to food waste like coffee grounds buried in a landfill?

- A) Nothing at all
- **✔ B) It rots without air and releases methane**
- C) It turns straight into clean soil

> Without air, food waste breaks down slowly and gives off methane, a strong greenhouse gas.

### 8. Singapore's only landfill, on Pulau Semakau, is expected to be full by about…

- **✔ A) 2035**
- B) 2100
- C) It will never fill up

> At today's rate Semakau Landfill is expected to fill up around 2035. Reusing things helps it last longer.

### 9. In "reduce, reuse, recycle", which comes first because it stops waste before it starts?

- A) Recycle
- B) Reuse
- **✔ C) Reduce**

> Reducing means the waste is never made. Reusing comes next, and recycling is the last of the three.

### 10. Your coffee paint looks too pale. What should you do?

- A) Add more water
- **✔ B) Steep more grounds in it**
- C) Put it in the fridge

> More grounds in the same water makes a stronger, darker brown.

### 11. A circular economy tries to…

- A) Only sell round objects
- B) Make things, use them once, then throw them away
- **✔ C) Keep materials in use for as long as possible**

> In a circular economy products and materials are reused, repaired and recycled, so very little becomes waste. The opposite is a linear "take, make, throw away" economy.

### 12. Why are used coffee grounds good to add to a compost bin?

- A) They make the compost smell like coffee
- **✔ B) They add nitrogen-rich material**
- C) They stop other waste from rotting

> Grounds count as a "green" compost material because they are rich in nitrogen, even though they look brown. Mixed with dry "brown" material they help the tiny microbes that make compost.

### 13. Which of these is a renewable source of energy?

- A) Natural gas
- B) Coal
- **✔ C) Solar power**

> Sunlight arrives every day, so it will not run out. Coal and gas took millions of years to form and we are using them up much faster than they form.

### 14. Counting everything from the farm to the cup, about how much water does one cup of coffee use?

- **✔ A) About 140 litres**
- B) About 1 litre
- C) About 2 spoonfuls

> Most of it is the water used to grow the coffee plant. This hidden "water footprint" is a reminder that everyday things cost resources before we ever see them.

### 15. What should you NOT do with leftover wet coffee grounds?

- **✔ A) Wash a lot of them down the sink**
- B) Dry them out before storing them
- C) Spread a thin layer on soil

> Grounds clump together and can block pipes. Put them in compost or dry them for projects instead.

### 16. What is the BEST way to deal with food waste?

- A) Burn it
- B) Bury it in a landfill
- **✔ C) Stop it being wasted in the first place**

> Preventing waste saves the water, energy and land that went into the food. Composting is good, but not wasting it is better.

### 17. Turning old jeans into a bag is an example of…

- A) Pollution
- **✔ B) Upcycling**
- C) Composting

> Upcycling turns something unwanted into something new that is just as useful, or more valuable. Recycling usually breaks a thing down into raw material first.

### 18. How many Sustainable Development Goals did the United Nations set for 2030?

- A) 70
- B) 7
- **✔ C) 17**

> The 17 goals cover things like clean water, climate action and responsible consumption, and all countries agreed to work on them.

### 19. Why can coffee be used as a natural paint?

- A) It is made from paint
- B) It contains glue
- **✔ C) Its brown colour stains paper**

> Many plants make coloured compounds that stain whatever they touch. People have used coffee and tea as dyes for hundreds of years.

### 20. What is a carbon footprint?

- A) The weight of carbon in your body
- B) A mark a shoe leaves in the soil
- **✔ C) The greenhouse gases caused by an activity or product**

> It adds up the greenhouse gases made by things like travel, food and products, so we can see which choices cause more climate change.

### 21. What does "closed loop" mean for materials?

- **✔ A) Waste from one process becomes the input for another**
- B) A factory with its doors closed
- C) A recycling bin with a lid

> In a closed loop nothing is thrown away: the leftovers of one process become the raw material of the next, like grounds becoming compost.

### 22. Which sustainable habit could you try this week?

- A) Bring my own bottle or cup
- B) Switch off lights and fans when I leave a room
- C) Finish all the food on my plate

*Reflection question: there is no wrong answer.*

> Any of these helps. Small habits add up, especially when a whole class does them together.

### 23. Why does Singapore burn most of its rubbish in waste-to-energy plants before using Semakau Landfill?

- A) It is the cheapest way to recycle plastic
- B) It makes the waste disappear with no side effects
- **✔ C) It shrinks the waste a lot, and the heat makes electricity**

> Burning cuts the volume of waste so the landfill lasts longer. The ash still goes to Semakau, so making less waste in the first place matters most.

### 24. Where else can used coffee grounds go instead of the bin?

- **✔ A) Mushroom farms and compost makers**
- B) Swimming pools
- C) Aeroplane engines

> Mushrooms grow well on grounds, and compost makers and biofuel makers use them too. Treating grounds as a resource instead of waste is circular thinking.

### 25. Microplastics are…

- **✔ A) Plastic pieces smaller than 5 mm**
- B) Plastic that dissolves in water
- C) Plastic made from plants

> They come from bigger plastics breaking down, and from tiny beads in some products. They end up in oceans, in animals and even in our food.

### 26. SDG 12 is about…

- A) Faster internet
- **✔ B) Responsible consumption and production**
- C) Exploring space

> It asks us to make and use things in ways that waste less, which is what reducing, reusing and recycling are all about.

### 27. A company calls its bottle "eco-friendly" but gives no proof. This could be…

- **✔ A) Greenwashing**
- B) Upcycling
- C) Composting

> Greenwashing is making something sound greener than it really is. Look for real facts, numbers and trusted labels.

### 28. Why can repairing a phone help the planet more than recycling it?

- A) Phones cannot be recycled
- B) Repaired phones use no electricity
- **✔ C) The whole product keeps working, so no new materials or energy are needed**

> Recycling breaks a product down and then needs energy to make a new one. Repairing keeps everything that is already made in use.

### 29. Before putting a plastic bottle in the recycling bin, you should…

- **✔ A) Empty and rinse it**
- B) Leave some drink inside
- C) Fill it with water

> Food and drink left inside can dirty other recyclables, and a dirty batch may not get recycled at all.

### 30. Why is a coffee-ground scrub good to use gently on hands?

- A) Grounds are sticky
- B) Grounds dissolve completely in water
- **✔ C) The tiny gritty bits lift dead skin without being sharp**

> Rub lightly and rinse well. Do a small patch test first, and never use a scrub on cuts or sore skin.

### 31. Which of these is an example of a linear economy?

- A) Turning grounds into compost
- B) Repairing a broken bicycle
- **✔ C) Make a product, use it once, then throw it away**

> A linear economy goes in a straight line from taking resources to making waste. A circular economy brings things back around.

### 32. The Paris Agreement aims to keep global warming…

- **✔ A) Well below 2°C, and ideally 1.5°C, above pre-industrial levels**
- B) At exactly today's level, with no limit
- C) Below 20°C

> Countries agreed on these limits in 2015 because every extra bit of warming makes floods, heatwaves and storms worse.

### 33. What is a "carbon sink"?

- A) A pipe that carries smoke
- **✔ B) Something that absorbs more carbon dioxide than it releases, like a forest**
- C) A sink full of coal

> Forests, oceans and soil soak up carbon dioxide. Protecting them helps slow climate change.

### 34. Why do many disposable coffee cups end up in the bin instead of being recycled?

- **✔ A) A thin plastic lining is hard to separate from the paper**
- B) Cups are not allowed in recycling by law
- C) The cups are made of metal

> The lining keeps the drink in, but it makes recycling harder. A reusable cup avoids the problem.

### 35. "Net zero" emissions means…

- A) Emissions are cut by half
- B) No greenhouse gas is ever made
- **✔ C) Greenhouse gases added are balanced by gases taken out**

> Some emissions are hard to avoid, so net zero balances what we add with what forests, soil or technology remove.

### 36. A "biodegradable" plastic bag will always rot quickly in a normal bin. True or false?

- **✔ A) False: many need warm, industrial composting conditions**
- B) True
- C) True, but only in water

> Biodegradable does not mean it vanishes anywhere. Check the label, and the best bag is the one you reuse.

### 37. Which of these is a "brown" material in a compost bin?

- **✔ A) Dry leaves**
- B) Coffee grounds
- C) Fruit peels

> Browns are dry and carbon-rich, like dry leaves and cardboard. Greens are wet and nitrogen-rich, like peels and coffee grounds. A good compost needs both.

### 38. A "life cycle" view of a coffee cup looks at…

- **✔ A) Every stage, from making the materials to throwing it away**
- B) Just the day you buy it
- C) Only what it looks like

> Looking at the whole life cycle shows where the biggest impact is. For many products, that is the making, not the throwing away.

### 39. Products made to wear out quickly so people buy new ones are an example of…

- A) Closed-loop design
- **✔ B) Planned obsolescence**
- C) Renewable energy

> It makes more waste. Choosing products that last, or can be repaired, pushes companies to build things better.

### 40. Why can eating local food sometimes help the environment?

- A) Local food has no packaging
- **✔ B) It may travel a shorter distance and use less fuel**
- C) It grows without any water

> Less transport can mean fewer emissions, but how the food is grown matters too, so it is only one part of the picture.

### 41. Singapore imports most of its food. What is the "30 by 30" goal?

- A) Plant 30 trees in 30 days
- B) Cut all waste by 30% in 30 years
- **✔ C) Grow 30% of our nutritional needs locally by 2030**

> Growing more food at home makes Singapore less dependent on imports, and a shorter journey can also mean fewer emissions.

### 42. Sharing a power drill with your neighbours, instead of everyone buying one, is part of…

- A) Planned obsolescence
- **✔ B) The sharing economy**
- C) Greenwashing

> Most drills are used for only minutes in their whole life. Sharing means fewer things need to be made.

### 43. What is e-waste?

- **✔ A) Old electronics like phones, chargers and laptops**
- B) Waste made only by electricity
- C) Emails you delete

> E-waste holds metals worth recovering, and harmful materials that must not go in the normal bin. Use proper e-waste collection points.

### 44. Recycling a plastic bottle into a lower-quality product, like a plastic bench, is sometimes called…

- A) Overcycling
- **✔ B) Downcycling**
- C) Upcycling

> The material loses quality each time it is recycled, so it can only be recycled a few times. That is why reducing and reusing come first.

### 45. Which light bulb uses the least electricity for the same brightness?

- A) They all use the same
- **✔ B) An LED bulb**
- C) An old-style filament bulb

> LEDs turn most of their energy into light, not heat. They also last much longer, so fewer bulbs are thrown away.

### 46. Why is wasting food also wasting other things?

- A) It only wastes the packaging
- B) Food waste is free to make
- **✔ C) Water, energy and land were used to grow and move it**

> When a meal is thrown away, so is everything used to grow, process, transport and cook it.

### 47. Which question could you ask before buying something new?

- A) Do I really need it?
- B) Can I borrow, repair or buy it second-hand?
- C) Will I still use it in a year?

*Reflection question: there is no wrong answer.*

> All three are good questions. Asking before you buy is the "reduce" in reduce, reuse, recycle.

### 48. Which habit saves the most water at home?

- **✔ A) Taking shorter showers**
- B) Leaving the tap running while you brush
- C) Washing a half-empty load every day

> Showers and washing use a lot of water. Small changes repeated every day add up to a big saving.

### 49. Why does clearing rainforest to grow crops harm the planet?

- **✔ A) Many animals and plants lose their homes**
- B) Trees get too much sunlight
- C) The soil gets too much shade

> Rainforests hold huge numbers of species. Shade-grown coffee, grown under trees, can protect more wildlife than clearing the land.

### 50. CoffeeBot collects grounds from offices and sends them to schools. This is an example of…

- **✔ A) A circular economy loop**
- B) A linear economy
- C) Greenwashing

> Grounds that would have been thrown away become a material for projects and compost, so they stay useful instead of ending up in landfill.
